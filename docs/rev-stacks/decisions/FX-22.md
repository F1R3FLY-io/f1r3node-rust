# FX-22: the gate commits a bound, not `phloLimit` (Decision 122)

Base: `patch/FX-20` at 91e3a02b7.

## What changed

- **The static estimate.** `rholang::accounting::delta_sigma::compute_bound(term, source_len, schedule)` gives the maximum compute charge of a term. `DeltaEstimate::of_source` combines it with the P3-01 COMM bound. `DeltaEstimate::bound` is the COMM bound plus the compute bound, or `None` when either is unbounded.
- **The gate.** `GateInput::static_bound` holds the estimate. `gate_inputs` computes it from the normalized term that the gate already reads. `BoundDemand` gives Δ = min(`phloLimit`, estimate), and Δ = `phloLimit` when there is no estimate. The executor, the replay check and the inclusion screen (`GateScreen`) use `BoundDemand`. The commitment on each paying wallet is Δ × `phloPrice`, as before.
- **The run.** A run and its replay evaluate with the phlo limit min(`phloLimit`, Δ). The record keeps the signed deployment unchanged. The settlement already caps each charge at Δ.

## The compute bound (working reading of "the cost schedule's maximum charge")

Decision 122 sums, over each node of the term, the cost schedule's maximum charge. Several charges in the schedule depend on the size of the data:
- produce and consume storage
- substitution
- equality checks
- string, byte and bigint operations.

The working reading gives such a charge a maximum only when the sizes are bounded by the term:

1. **The fragment.** The estimate exists only if all of these are true:
   - P3-01 gives a COMM bound, so the term is closed and linear and does not use system channels.
   - Each variable bound by a receive or a match case occurs at most once in its body (affine data).
   - The term has no receive conditions, no match guards, no `new` injections and no token stacks.
   - Each expression is a ground literal (bool, int, string, URI, byte array), a variable, a collection (list, tuple, set, map), or one of `not`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `%`, `+`, `-`, `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`, `==`, `!=`, `and`, `or`.
   - The term has no method calls, `++`, `--` or `%%`, and no bigint, bigrat, double or fixed-point values.
2. **Why the sizes are bounded.** In the fragment no value is duplicated, and no operation grows a value past its operands. A private name that replaces a variable grows that occurrence by a constant factor. So every value that a receive binds is at most `name_expansion` × S + `data_slack`, where S is the encoded size of the term.
3. **The charge of a node.** Each node (send, receive, `new`, match, `if`, bundle, signed term, expression) adds:

   `node_base` + `per_bind` × binds + `size_factor` × (`name_expansion` × node size + D)

   D is the data bound from item 2 when the node is under a data binder, and 0 when it is not. The compute bound is the source length (the parsing charge) plus the sum over all nodes. A linear term evaluates each node at most once.
4. **Configuration.** `ScheduleBound` holds the constants. The defaults are `node_base` 256, `per_bind` 128, `size_factor` 4, `name_expansion` 8 and `data_slack` 64.

Measured on the corpus (`delta_sigma_spec`), the estimate is 15 to 170 times the phlo that the run spends. A property test on 64 generated affine terms with data flow found no run that spends more than its estimate.

## Decisions

### D1. The estimate exists only for the affine fragment with fixed-cost expressions

- **Decision:** as in the working reading above.
- **Alternatives:**
  - (i) Propagate a size bound per name through at most C rounds (C is the COMM bound). This admits duplicated variables, at the cost of bounds that grow with each round.
  - (ii) Use the measured costs of the operations and not the schedule.
- **Reason:** an estimate that is too low makes an honest deployment run out of phlo, because the run's limit is the bound. The fragment gives a bound that follows from the schedule, with a simple argument.
- **Reversible:** yes.

### D2. The constants are conservative and are not tuned down

- **Decision:** the `ScheduleBound` defaults above.
- **Alternatives:** fit the constants to the measured runs.
- **Reason:** a fitted constant could fall below a run that the corpus does not cover. A charging decision about how tight the bound is belongs to Meredith.
- **Reversible:** yes. `ScheduleBound` is configuration.

### D3. The run's limit is applied to a copy of the deployment

- **Decision:** the run and the replay evaluate a copy whose `phloLimit` is Δ. The processed deployment in the record is the signed one.
- **Alternatives:** add a phlo-limit parameter to `process_deploy_with_mergeable_data` and `replay_deploy_e`.
- **Reason:** those are dev's signatures with many callers. In the fragment no system process reads the deployment data, and outside the fragment Δ is `phloLimit`, so the copy is identical.
- **Reversible:** yes.

## Decision 88 labels

- *Known issue:* the estimate is loose (15 to 170 times the measured cost). Reason: D2. A deployment in the fragment still commits much less than a typical `phloLimit`.
- *Known issue:* terms outside the fragment commit `phloLimit`, which includes every term that calls a system contract. Reason: Decision 122 gives `phloLimit` for unbounded terms. Most deployments that touch the registry or a wallet are outside the fragment.
